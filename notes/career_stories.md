# Career Stories

## The "Decoy Dummy"

While working at Whittman-Hart / MarchFirst on the PlanetFeedback.com project, I was introduced to Vignette StoryServer and the Tcl language — technologies I had never used before.

My employer trusted my ability to learn new systems quickly enough that they sent me to an intensive three-week course before immediately integrating me into the project.

One of my teammates and I frequently bounced problems and ideas off each other while debugging and designing solutions. Long before pair programming became mainstream, we jokingly called each other "decoy dummies" because simply explaining a problem out loud often led us to solve it ourselves.

Years later, we still joke that modern AI tools now serve as an evolved version of the "decoy dummy" — collaborative reasoning partners that help accelerate problem-solving and idea exploration.

## Lotus Notes and the Beginning of Development

While working in Reno as a network technician for Miller Heiman, I became fascinated with Lotus Notes and began teaching myself Lotus Notes formulas and LotusScript.

At the time, the company relied heavily on paper versions of its Strategic Selling and Conceptual Selling systems. I independently developed electronic versions of those systems, which sparked my long-term passion for software development and systems design.

That experience ultimately led me to pursue software development professionally.

---

## The Cincinnati Interview

After deciding to transition into software development full time, I searched nationally for opportunities and interviewed with Synergistics in Cincinnati.

While waiting for the formal interview, I noticed developers nearby struggling with a technical problem and joined the conversation to help troubleshoot it.

Later that same day, I received the call that I had been hired.

---

## Becoming a Technical Author

While working at Reach Publishing, the company decided to transition away from Lotus Notes and asked me to identify a new technology direction.

After extensive research, I recommended PHP and immersed myself in learning the platform through online communities, experimentation, and collaborative troubleshooting.

As my expertise grew, I became increasingly active helping other developers solve problems online — an experience that reinforced how much teaching helped deepen my own understanding.

That visibility eventually led Wiley Publishing to approach me about leading a collaborative authoring effort for a PHP development book series.
