# Resume and LinkedIn Process Plan

## Current Goal

Build a professional profile, resume set, and LinkedIn presence that feels like Michael: experienced, practical, curious, human, and distinct without becoming gimmicky.

The goal is not to get the maximum number of interviews from the widest possible audience. The goal is to find the right fit: a company or team that sees the value in a tool-agnostic, UX-focused systems builder who likes messy problems and knows how to make software useful for real people.

---

## Current Positioning

Michael is a tool-agnostic application developer with a strong front-end and UX focus.

Core themes:

- Front-end modernization
- Internal tools and operational systems
- Legacy application modernization
- Workflow design
- UX-focused application development
- Performance troubleshooting
- Rapid technical learning
- Practical architecture
- Technical writing and teaching
- AI-assisted workflows used thoughtfully

Working summary:

> A systems-oriented application developer with a strong UX/front-end focus who specializes in modernization, internal tools, workflow systems, and solving messy technical problems.

---

## Voice and Style

Use Michael's writing voice:

- Direct
- Conversational
- Practical
- Specific
- Lightly dry when appropriate
- Professional without sounding embalmed
- Human, not corporate-polished
- Confident without peacocking

Avoid:

- Generic LinkedIn language
- Buzzword stuffing
- Motivational poster phrasing
- AI-polished transitions
- Overly tidy corporate summaries
- Fake warmth
- Inflated claims

The writing should feel like a real developer with judgment, experience, and taste. Not like a committee wrote it after three rounds of brand alignment.

## Resume Versions

### `resume_master.md`

Purpose: source of truth.

This file can be longer and more detailed. It is where facts, language, project details, and positioning live before being trimmed into usable versions.

### `resume_profile.md`

Purpose: the distinctive version.

This is the “right company gets me” resume. It has more voice, more narrative, and more emphasis on how Michael thinks and works.

Best for:

- LinkedIn Featured section
- Direct outreach
- Thoughtful companies
- Smaller teams
- Civic tech / internal tools / UX-conscious roles
- Situations where a human is likely to read carefully

### `resume_conventional.md`

Purpose: the safer application version.

This version is more structured, recruiter-friendly, and easier for job systems to parse.

Best for:

- Job applications
- LinkedIn Easy Apply
- ATS-heavy portals
- Recruiters who need quick scanning
- More traditional employers

Important note: these are not different identities. They are different tools for different rooms. Same person, different packaging.

## Explanation for Multiple Resume Versions

Simple explanation:

> I’m not creating multiple identities. I’m creating different tools for different situations.
>
> One version is the fuller “this is who I am and how I think” profile. That’s the one meant to stand out to the right company.
>
> Another version is more straightforward and easier for job sites and recruiters to scan quickly.
>
> They both say the same thing. They just package it differently depending on who, or what, is reading it.

More casual version:

> It’s like dressing for the room. Same person, different presentation.
>
> Some companies want a clean resume they can scan fast. Others respond better to something with more personality and story. I’m making both so I can use the right one in the right situation instead of trying to make one document do everything.
